# HEADNULL60 worst-vocabulary-row nulling for the NF4 sham

A deterministic trajectory removed up to ten high-response vocabulary rows
from the continuous local sham atlas before selecting a direction. Every
world first selected the same vocabulary row. Its identity remains in the
private receipt. The method lowered the median predicted peak from 0.859
to 0.119 on fit and from 0.863 to 0.121 on the disjoint CAL check.

| Panel | Full four-arm passes | Full passes with both signed RMS gaps ≤0.005 | Both-world roots with full and RMS gates |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Fit | 6/8 worlds | 2/8 worlds | 1/4 roots |
| Disjoint CAL check | 5/8 worlds | 0/8 worlds | 0/4 roots |

All 16 worlds passed the physical dose and contrast gates. The finite
head rejected two fit worlds and three check worlds on the sham's peak
absolute logit shift. The unrestricted greedy winner and bare-label margin
gates passed for all signed shams. The median actual sham peak was 0.125
in both panels. The two RMS-matched fit worlds had worst-sign RMS gaps of
0.00227 and 0.00432. In the disjoint check's five full passes, median
active RMS was 0.01781 and median sham RMS was 0.02858.

The LOWHEAD59 chord passed 4/8 worlds on fit and 5/8 on check, with zero RMS
matches. Row nulling recovered one both-world fit root with matched RMS.
The disjoint panel retained the head-profile gap. The result applies
to the 24-dimensional atlas and fixed search of up to ten rows.

The first synthetic fixture assumed its starting eigenvector would violate
the peak threshold. Its overlapping columns produced a lower peak after
eigenvector mixing. A disjoint-column fixture now verifies the row-exclusion
path. The original failed source seal was preserved locally before archive
readout. The successful replay streamed the frozen full head for 80 clean
or edited states, with zero new decoder calls and about 463 MiB peak sampled
private memory. The unit is the exposed source root. No formal tests or
