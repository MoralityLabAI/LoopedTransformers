## What a matched intervention changes

Matching the reached midpoint and geometric dose was insufficient for the first swap shams. The exact-coordinate-magnitude family in HEADSWAP107 missed the 0.005-logit differential RMS tolerance on all 100 signed development pairs. Redistributing the same midpoint-centered displacement across coordinates in CENTERSHAM108 matched both differential and common first-pass RMS for all 100, including on the 165,632 vocabulary rows excluded from selection. Adding greedy-token, peak-drift and paired-margin checks left **44/44 NF4** and **52/56 BF16** pairs qualified. Nine of ten common roots qualified in both precisions, with both signs in both worlds. These checks constrain head magnitudes and behavioral readouts at the edit site. They leave the directions of the vocabulary shifts free.

READERREPLAY112 applied the pinned BF16 normalizations and layer-0 Q/K/V projections to the same sealed endpoints. The fitted T1-to-T2 chord produced **0.916×** the sham’s dose-normalized K half-chord response across the 11 fully qualified BF16 roots. The active K gain was smaller on each of those roots. Q and V ratios were 0.935 and 0.988. This CPU replay stops at the pre-RoPE projection. Test whether the direction of the K change matters for the future answer even when the sham moves K farther. The registered native contrast compares active and sham margins across passes and restores the exact clean K while preserving Q, V and the edited residual. A rescue effect on the oracle-graded answer is still unmeasured.

The Inkling work motivates matched-depth evaluation and targeted reader measurements. Inkling’s dedicated cross-loop attention and Nanbeige’s serial residual carry require architecture-specific hooks. The TRM observer setup provides the complementary distinction between the current readout and the state that influences later outputs. For Nanbeige, preserve post-rounding dose and the full first-pass head, then measure the actual delayed change in a named reader and verified answer. [Inkling 2 Mini](https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-mini/), [Goodfire reader and lens analysis](https://www.goodfire.com/research/interpreting-inkling-2-mini/), [TRM observer protocol](../../mechinterp/trm_roc01/PROTOCOL.md)

## Device status and research decisions

- **Fit comparison.** Hold the checkpoint, train/CAL roots, site, rank, optimizer, budget, replacement rule and physical dose fixed. Compare SVD, static routing, first-pass VPD-style fitting and recurrent BPD on the same future readout. Report native all-one closure, actual rounded writes, full-head perturbations, paired repairs and regressions.
- **PercInterp calibration.** Measure positive and negative finite steps at the unrolled occurrences. Use the full vocabulary to define the behavioral sink, check stability under root omission, and freeze comparable per-phase masks before the native comparison. Keep oracle accuracy and the signed path effects side by side.
- **Native rescue pilot.** BOUNDRESCUE109 has 16 fresh development roots, 96 prompts and 48 paired baselines. Its staged CPU fixture verified 32 edited or rescued continuations and 16 exact K restorations. The independent host fixture recovered the synthetic delayed effect and refused failed competence and altered receipts. Fresh native outcomes will determine the matched-sham variation and meaningful effect floor for a later confirmation set.
- **Streaming.** Keep one model on the 4 GiB device. Share phase-1 work and staged layer loads across edited and rescued B2 branches with independent caches. RESCUEGROUP110 reproduced all serial heads and native reader states in the CPU fixture and reduced stage copies from 228 to 90. That projects to about 221 GiB fewer requested BF16 parameter copies per qualified root. Hardware timing and residency remain unmeasured for the grouped lane. RESCUEGPU111 requires exact serial parity at position 391 before scored CUDA execution. At this report snapshot BF31 had closed at least 46 of 1,549 baselines and continued streaming paired evals.
